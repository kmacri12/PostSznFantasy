# Product rules

How PostSzn Fantasy is played. This is the source of truth for game behaviour; code and issues follow it. It comes from the commissioner's 15-year spreadsheet league plus a design interview, and anything marked **Open** is deliberately unsettled.

## 1. The game in one paragraph

Each league drafts once, before the Wild Card round, from players on the 14 playoff teams. Every round (Wild Card, Divisional, Conference Championship, Super Bowl) each owner sets a starting lineup from the drafted players whose teams are still alive. Starters score; the bench does not. One cumulative leaderboard decides the champion.

## 2. Draft

- Snake draft, one draft per league, 18 rounds by default (rounds equal the roster size).
- Only players on playoff teams are in the pool, and a draft cannot start until the playoff field is set.
- A draft cannot be scheduled or started after the first Wild Card kickoff. A draft already running may finish; players whose game has started are still draftable and lock on the bench for that round.
- Default 6 teams, 90-second pick clock. League size may be 2 to 14.
- Position caps (default QB 2, K 2, DST 2; RB, WR and TE uncapped). Once a team reaches a cap, that position's players are greyed out in its draft pool with a plain-language reason. Auto-pick (queue first, then the highest season fantasy points under the league's scoring) never picks an ineligible player. A team with no owner is auto-picked within a second of coming on the clock; a later joiner inherits its picks.
- Create-league shows a yellow warning when teams times max-per-position exceeds the 14 available units of a position (QB, K, DST).
- Roster slots, caps and team count are locked when the draft starts.

## 3. Roster and lineup

- Default roster: 1 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 1 TE, 1 FLEX (RB/WR/TE), 1 K, 1 DST, plus 9 bench (18 total).
- Empty slots are allowed and score 0.
- Fullbacks are running backs: ingest maps FB to RB and users never see FB anywhere.
- Lineups are per round and set manually. Nothing is copied forward, pre-filled or auto-substituted. After the draft, all players are unassigned.
- Lineup edits are open at any time, subject to the lock rules below.
- **Locks.** Each player locks at their game's **scheduled kickoff**, in whatever slot they are in (starter, flex or bench). A locked player cannot be moved, swapped or dropped. A move is legal only if every player involved is unlocked.
- **Eliminated players.** A player whose NFL team lost is shown in a separate Eliminated section below the bench, but only in the round right after the loss (per round: a Wild Card loser is still valid for the Wild Card and eliminated for the Divisional, and gone from the page in the Conference Championship). Each says which round it went out in. Eliminated players cannot be moved. When a round ends, eliminated players drop out of any slot they were placed in early, leaving it empty.
- **Byes.** A team on a bye is alive and its players score 0 that round.
- **Notifications.** Owners who turned push on get a message when their draft pick is on the clock and, once per round, about an hour before the round's first kickoff if a starter slot is empty and a player is left to fill it. Every other member of the league is also told when someone posts in the chat (the author and league name only, never the message, and a burst collapses into one notification); there is no per-member mute yet. iPhone requires the site on the Home Screen.
- Players not placed in a slot are listed with the bench and score nothing; placing a player on the bench is optional. Unplaced players count as bench for "points left on the bench". A player drafted after their game kicked off is locked out of the lineup, so their points that round are bench points.
- A banner on the team page warns about empty starter slots before the round's first kickoff. There is no email or push in v1.
- There is no free agency in v1. It may return later as an opt-in league setting.

## 4. Team QB and team K

Two independent league settings with a tooltip. The "Customize rules" path of create league shows both choices (off preselected). The short create path (league name and team count only) sets both off, standard individual QB and K; a commissioner can change them in settings at any time. The off default for the short path is a product decision awaiting the owner's confirmation.

- When on, the owner drafts a QB (or K) by name, but the slot scores every player whose official roster position is QB (or K) on that NFL team in that game. A WR who throws a touchdown pass scores as a WR.
- The draft pool lists one QB (or K) per NFL team, the starter; the whole team's QBs (or Ks) leave the pool when one is drafted. The starter is the first name on the NFL team's depth chart (QB1, PK1 from Tank01's `getNFLDepthCharts`); if a team has no usable entry the player with the most pass attempts (or kicks) in the latest season stats starts, and a warning is logged. The roster, lineup and draft board show the drafted name with a tooltip such as "scores all PHI QBs".
- Who counts as a QB or K is the player's official roster position on that NFL team (Tank01 calls kickers `PK`). A position cap then means different NFL teams: with the setting on, a team can never draft two QBs from one NFL team.
- The settings are changed on the league settings page by a commissioner at any time, including after the draft; every change goes to the activity feed.
- DST is always a team unit.
- Switching on after the draft is blocked while any team holds two QBs (or Ks) from the same NFL team, and the conflicts are listed. Switching off is always allowed.
- Positions other than QB and K never combine (a backup RB is an ordinary player).

## 5. Scoring

- Defaults are full PPR with the commissioner's spreadsheet values; every item is commissioner-editable, and everything not listed below is 0 (completion, attempt and yardage bonuses, and so on).
- Passing: 0.04 per yard, 4 per TD, -2 per interception. Rushing and receiving: 0.1 per yard, 6 per TD. Reception: 1. Fumble lost: -2. Two-point conversion: 2.
- Kicker: 0.1 per made field-goal yard, 1 per extra point, -1 per missed field goal, -1 per missed extra point. No distance tiers. Optional, off by default: a rate per yard of each missed field goal (e.g. -0.05), from the play-by-play distances.
- DST: sack 1, interception 1, fumble recovery 1, safety 2, TD 6, defensive two-point return 2, blocked kick (punt, field goal or extra point, one setting) 1. Kick and punt return TDs always go to the DST, never to the individual returner. Yards allowed: 0-99 = 6, 100-199 = 4, 200-299 = 2, 300+ = 0. Points allowed: 0 = 7, 1-13 = 5, 14-21 = 3, 22-28 = 1, 29+ = 0. Points allowed counts only what the defense gave up: the opponent's final score minus 6 for each touchdown the opponent's own defense or special teams scored (so a pick-six thrown by the defense's own offense is not charged to it). Yards allowed is the opponent's total offensive yards.
- A round's score is the sum of points from players in starter slots, multiplied by that round's multiplier (default 1x for all four, commissioner-editable).
- Scores always recompute from the latest data and settings. A stat correction or settings change reflows every round it touches; nobody edits scores by hand.

## 6. Standings and ties

- One cumulative leaderboard; no head-to-head.
- Rank order uses the tiebreaker chain, starting with the latest completed round: the higher score in that round wins, then the previous round, back to the Wild Card. If every round ties, the team with more rostered players (starters and bench) on the Super Bowl winner ranks higher; if that ties, they share the title.
- Before the draft has produced standings, the league home lists every team slot of the season instead (`phase: pre_draft`): filled rows with team, owner and a commissioner badge, "Open spot" rows, a dash instead of points and rank, and the line "Waiting for the draft: X of N teams filled." Rows fill in as people join. The commissioner's invite panel sits directly above while the league is open. Once the snapshot exists (`phase: live`) the normal standings take over.
- Wherever a tie decided the order, a tooltip says why one team is above the other.
- Players left is the number of players on the roster, starters or not, whose NFL team is still alive. A league-wide total is shown too and updates as soon as a team is eliminated.
- The home page updates live during games. Each team shows its points, plus nine starters (or however many) as finished, live, to-play and not-playing (colour-coded), and the number of games in progress. Six teams must be visible at once on a phone: rank, team and total in the collapsed row, with the round breakdown, players left and live counts on expand.
- Every team can show its round-by-round points, with each starter's counted points and the bench's points, muted, and a team total of points left on the bench. Bench points are calculated on read from the lineup, game stats and league scoring (nothing is stored) and use the round's multiplier like counted points, so the two compare directly.
- The live counts describe the current round: the first round not yet complete (every game final), or the Super Bowl once it is final. A starter slot is exactly one of finished, live, to play or not playing (empty slot, eliminated player or bye). The count of games in progress is the number of NFL games being played now.
- Teams level on every step of the chain share a rank, shown as "T1" and so on. The chain only uses completed rounds and the Super Bowl winner once that game is final.

## 7. Commissioner powers and settings

- A commissioner may edit any team's lineup at any time, including locked slots and past rounds. Locked-slot or past-round edits require an explicit "this is a commissioner-only move and the league will be notified" confirmation, and are marked prominently afterwards ("changed by commissioner after kickoff"). Slots set by a commissioner and still unlocked carry an asterisk with who and when. An owner may overwrite an unlocked slot; owners can only make valid unlocked moves.
- A league always has at least one commissioner. A team whose owner leaves stays exactly as it was; the commissioner can manage it or assign it to a new member.
- Roster slots, caps and team count lock at draft start. Scoring, multipliers and the team QB/K choices stay editable; changes recompute everything. Owner decision (#333): they stay editable for the whole season, with no lock at the first Wild Card kickoff. Once any game is live or final, the settings page asks the commissioner to confirm ("This re-scores every team's finished rounds and changes standings") before saving; the Feed event stays. Numeric settings are validated per field (blank or half-typed values are never saved as 0; multipliers 0 to 10, roster slots whole numbers 0 to 20, position caps 0 to 99, scoring values -100 to 100, the same bounds the API enforces) and leaving the page with unsaved edits asks first.
- Commissioner notes: a collapsed, free-text card at the bottom of the League tab for house rules and a welcome message.
- Deleting a league (any commissioner, anytime, finished or not, #231): a soft delete of the permanent league, so it covers every season and no row is removed. It is refused (409) while a draft in any season is in progress or paused, and the request must carry the league's exact name (422 otherwise). It is permanent for users: no restore in the app; only the owner's `python -m api.scripts.restore_league <id>` brings a league back. A deleted league leaves the league list, switcher, nav dot and activity feeds; its invite is revoked and answers 404 (the code is never reissued); the draft clock, standings recompute and scheduler skip it; awards and recaps do not include it. Members and former members who open a link to it get 404 with code `leagueDeleted` (name and deletion date only); anyone else gets the same 403 a live league gives strangers, so ids cannot be probed. Its predecessor stays "already run back". A user with only deleted leagues sees the normal empty landing page. In the app, Delete league sits at the bottom of a season's settings for commissioners and always takes the same three steps: a warning that the delete is permanent and cannot be undone, with the number of seasons it removes; typing the league name exactly; and a separate "Are you sure?" screen whose delete button is not where the previous step's button was.

## 8. Activity feed

One feed per league and one per team, collapsed by default. It records lineup moves, commissioner edits, stat corrections (old score, new score, time), settings changes (old and new values) and league membership: the league being created, members joining (with the team they took), leaving or being removed, and commissioner role changes (who did it). Membership belongs to the league but is posted to the league's current season feed; a new season starts a fresh feed and does not repost carried-over members. Leagues that existed before these events were logged got them reconstructed once from stored timestamps (the creator is the first member to join; a removal shows as that member leaving). A small dot on the league nav and on the feed section marks new activity and clears when the feed is expanded. Individual player scores that changed also carry a tooltip.

The league feed lists every event in the league, and a team's feed the events about that team (settings changes belong to the league only). Commissioner overrides, stat corrections, settings changes and membership joins, leaves and role changes light the league dot (nav and feed section); league creation does not; ordinary owner lineup moves appear in the feed without it, while a team's own feed counts them. A member's own actions never count as new for them, and activity from before they joined is not new, except membership events, so someone who has just joined sees the dot on their first visit and learns the feed exists. A commissioner move that touches a locked player is an override (confirmation, a permanent marker, a league-feed entry); one that touches only unlocked players is an ordinary move marked with an asterisk until the player locks or an owner overwrites the slot. A stat correction is a changed score for a rostered player in a game that was already final, per league because scoring differs. With team QB or team K on, a correction to any QB or K of the unit is a change to the drafted player's score and is posted against him.

### Feed and chat (Epic 21)

The league's Feed tab merges the season's events with chat, newest first, one stream and one unread dot. Lineup moves are still stored (slot marks and the team feed derive from them) but are not shown in the Feed and never light the dot. Draft events are posted with the action: a commissioner randomizing the draft order posts the full resulting order; changing it by hand posts who moved from which position to which (a save that moves nobody posts nothing); starting the draft and the last pick (completed, no actor) post too. All four count toward the dot.

Chat v1 is text (up to 2000 characters) and GIFs; no threads, edits or reactions. A GIF is a link to a host on the `GIF_ALLOWED_HOSTS` allowlist (https only). Members post only to the league's current season, so an archived season's Feed is read-only (409), and a former member cannot post. Authors delete their own messages and any commissioner deletes any; a deleted message disappears from the Feed and no longer counts as new, but the row is kept. A message is new for everyone except its author, unless it predates their joining; one viewed marker per member and season clears events and messages together.

## 9. Explaining the game

Several rules are unfamiliar (team QB/K, per-round lineups, kickoff locks, eliminated players). Explain them where they matter: a How to play page in the nav, the empty pre-draft roster showing every slot with its caps and the lock rules, tooltips on commissioner settings, and a "locks at kickoff" note on the lineup screen. All of this copy lives in `frontend/src/content/rules.ts`. League notes are plain text of up to 5000 characters on the league page, which only commissioners edit; edits are not logged to the activity feed.

## 10. Season end and history

- A league is permanent and has one season per year (`league`, `year` is unique). The league holds its name, notes, members and roles, and the invite; a season holds its settings, number of teams, teams and owners, draft, rosters, lineups, standings, activity feed, awards and recap.
- A season is finished when its NFL season's Super Bowl is final. That is derived from the games, so nothing is stored and nothing can drift. A league is never read-only; only its finished seasons are.
- The champion is the team ranked first in the final standings (the same tiebreaker chain as section 6). Teams level on the whole chain are co-champions. The league page becomes a recap: champion(s) first, then the awards.
- Awards (all use counted points, which means points scored while in a starter slot, multiplied) are computed on read, never stored: Playoff MVP (most counted points), Champion's MVP (most counted points on the champion's team), top player at each position that scored, best single game (one player, one round), best round (one team, one round), most points left on the bench (the team's total bench points, the same calculation as the round breakdown) and the per-position draft awards below. Ties on an award go to the lower player or team id; for a draft award, to the earlier pick.
- Draft value, per position (QB, RB, WR, TE, K, DST): the order the player was drafted among his position's picks, minus his finish rank among that position's drafted players by counted points (bench points do not count; players who never scored count as 0; level players share the better rank). Places gained is the difference, so a WR taken 32nd among WRs who finished 11th is +21. Each position gets a best value (highest) and a biggest bust (lowest), 12 awards in all; there is no cutoff at zero, so a best value can be negative, and no overall pair. A card reads "Drafted as WR32, finished WR11, +21 places" with the points. With team QB / team K on, the pick is scored as the whole unit's counted points (what the standings use), attributed to the drafted player.
- A finished season is a permanent read-only archive: it can be read by the league's members and every write to it (lineups, picks, settings, team count, team owners, team renames) is refused with 409. Season responses carry `canEdit` (open season and active member) and `canCommission` (that, as a commissioner). The league itself keeps working between seasons: renames, notes, members, roles and the invite change as ever. A league's list entry shows its latest season and a "Season over" tag once that season is over, with a Past seasons list under it.
- Start a season: a commissioner of a league whose latest season is finished starts the next one (year + 1), but only from December 1 of the calendar year of that season's Super Bowl, the next season's own year (Super Bowl in February 2027: December 1, 2027); earlier the API answers 409 and the overview's `canStartSeason` is false, and the finished season stays the league's home. The date comes from the Super Bowl's kickoff, or the season's year + 1 when no kickoff is stored. The new season takes the teams (names and owners) and settings the last one ended with, fresh rosters and an unscheduled, empty draft. The members and the invite code do not change. There is one open season at a time, and the commissioner may change the team count before the new draft starts. A league can be created only for a season that is not over yet.
- Membership history: leaving or being removed sets `left_at` and keeps the row. A season's teams and owners are permanent once it is finished, so a member who owned a team in a season they finished stays credited in its standings, awards and recap. A former member can read the seasons they finished, read-only, but not the current season, the notes, the member list or the invite, and cannot draft or set lineups. A member without a team in a season can read the league but not draft or set a lineup. Leaving before the next season starts frees their team for a replacement; leaving mid-season leaves the team unclaimed with its roster until a commissioner reassigns it, and whoever owns it at the Super Bowl is credited for the finish. Any member can leave from Settings, Leave league (the only commissioner must first make someone else commissioner). A former member who uses a live invite rejoins as a regular member, except that removing a member always revokes the league's invite link, so a removed person cannot return with the old link; the commissioner sees that the link is retired and regenerates it when they choose. Joining through an invite claims the lowest-numbered open team of the current season automatically (a commissioner can still reassign owners); with no open team the join is refused as "This league is full" (`league_full`) until the commissioner raises the team count. The public invite preview shows the league, every current member's display name (never emails), the open slots and whether the league is full.
- What each person sees: a league in the list shows its latest season and, once that season is over, "Season over: start 2027" (a button for commissioners, a note for members), plus its earlier seasons as Past seasons. A member who left finds the seasons they finished under "Leagues you left". Someone who joined between seasons has no team until a commissioner assigns one, and the league page says so. Teams that had no owner when a season ended credit nobody, and the finished season's page says so.

## Injury tags

Players show a tag (Out, Doubtful, Questionable, Injured Reserve) in the draft pool and the lineup when Tank01's player list reports one. It is display only: it never blocks a pick or a lineup move, and it is as fresh as the daily player sync. Source and caveats: [research/injury-status.md](research/injury-status.md).

## Out of scope for v1

Free agency, email or push reminders, any money handling (the app never handles, supports or facilitates money; Terms and How to play say so), and mid-playoff leagues.
